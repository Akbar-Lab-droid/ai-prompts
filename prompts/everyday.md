# Everyday Life

5 prompts. See [PROMPTING-GUIDE.md](../PROMPTING-GUIDE.md) for how to write your own.

## A Week of Meals From What's in My Kitchen

**Purpose:** Plan 7 simple dinners from ingredients you already have, plus a short shopping list.

```
Plan my meals for the week. I have these ingredients already: [list]. I cook for [number] people. Budget: [amount]. I don't eat [restrictions].

Give me 7 dinners that use what I have first, each under 30 minutes, with a short ingredient list. Then a shopping list of only what I'm missing. Keep the meals simple — real food, not restaurant dishes.
```

**Tip:** List what's actually in your fridge, not what you wish was there — the plan only works with real ingredients.

**What to expect:** Monday: fried rice with egg and the leftover chicken. Shopping list: eggs, spring onions, soy sauce.

## A Monthly Budget That Fits Real Life

**Purpose:** See where your money actually goes and find one realistic cut if the math doesn't add up.

```
Help me make a monthly budget. My income is [amount]. My fixed costs are: [rent, bills, etc.]. I want to save [amount or %].

Show me: where the money goes (in a simple table), what's left for food and fun, and one realistic cut if the numbers don't add up. Don't lecture me — just show the math and one small change I could try first.
```

**Tip:** Use last month's real spending, not what you think you spend — the gap is usually the whole problem.

**What to expect:** Income: 5,000,000. Rent 1,500,000, food 1,200,000... Left: 300,000. One change: cooking 3 more dinners a week saves about 400,000.

## Gift Ideas for Someone Hard to Shop For

**Purpose:** Get 5 gift ideas at different prices, each with a real reason it fits the person.

```
Help me find a gift for [who, e.g. my dad]. He's/she's [age], likes [interests], and already has [things they own]. Budget: [amount].

Give me 5 ideas at different prices, each with one sentence on why it fits them. No generic gift cards unless nothing else works. One idea should be something I can make or do, not buy.
```

**Tip:** The best clue is what they complain about or keep borrowing — gifts that solve a small annoyance get used.

**What to expect:** 1) A good thermos (he complains his coffee goes cold on the road). 5) Cook his favorite meal together — costs almost nothing, he'll remember it.

## Plan a Short Trip Without the Stress

**Purpose:** Get a relaxed day-by-day trip plan with costs, not a schedule packed with 6 stops a day.

```
Plan a [number]-day trip to [place] for [number] people. Budget: [amount] total. We like [interests] and want to avoid [things you dislike].

Give me: a day-by-day plan with 2-3 things per day (with rough times), where to stay (area, not a specific hotel), how to get around, and the total estimated cost. Keep the pace relaxed — no day packed with 6 stops.
```

**Tip:** Say what you actually enjoy — a plan built for 'sightseeing' when you hate museums will be miserable.

**What to expect:** Day 1: arrive, check in near Braga street, evening food walk. Day 2: morning at the tea plantation, afternoon free.

## Reply to a Difficult Message

**Purpose:** Write a reply to a hard message that sounds calm, human, and clear — in two lengths.

```
Help me reply to this message:

[Paste the message]

Context: [who it's from, what happened]. I want to sound [calm / firm / friendly] and I want [what outcome].

Give me a reply under 80 words, and one shorter version (under 30 words) if I want to keep it brief. Don't make it sound like a lawyer or a robot — write like a normal person.
```

**Tip:** Wait before sending a heated reply — write it, do something else for 10 minutes, then read it once more.

**What to expect:** Hi, thanks for letting me know. I understand the delay, but I need this by Friday or I'll have to find another option — can you confirm?
