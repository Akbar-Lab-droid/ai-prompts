# Writing

5 prompts. See [PROMPTING-GUIDE.md](../PROMPTING-GUIDE.md) for how to write your own.

## Rewrite This So It's Easy to Read

**Purpose:** Turn dense or confusing text into something people actually enjoy reading.

```
Rewrite this text so it's clear and easy to read. Keep the same meaning and the same facts — don't add anything new:

[Paste your text]

Use short sentences, everyday words, and one idea per paragraph. If a sentence is confusing, split it. Then list the 3 biggest changes you made.
```

**Tip:** Say who will read it — a rewrite for a customer sounds different from one for a coworker.

**What to expect:** Before: 'The implementation of the aforementioned methodology facilitates...' After: 'This method helps you get the work done faster.'

## Outline a Blog Post That People Finish

**Purpose:** Build a blog outline with a hook, clear sections, and a real ending.

```
Create an outline for a blog post about [topic]. The reader is [who they are] and they want [what they want to achieve].

Give me: a working title, a hook for the first paragraph, 4-6 main sections with one sentence each on what they cover, and a closing that tells the reader what to do next. Keep it practical — no filler sections.
```

**Tip:** A good outline names the reader's problem in the first line — that's what keeps them reading.

**What to expect:** Title: 'How I Cut My Grocery Bill by 30% Without Eating Worse' — Hook: a one-line story about the receipt that shocked me. Sections: 1) where the money actually goes...

## Write a Professional Email

**Purpose:** Write a short, polite email that gets to the point and gets a reply.

```
Write a professional email about [topic]. Context: [what happened, who it's to, what you need from them].

Keep it under 150 words. Start with the point, not the background. Tone: polite but direct. End with one clear question or next step. Give me a subject line too.
```

**Tip:** Put the ask in the first two sentences — busy people decide whether to keep reading that fast.

**What to expect:** Subject: Request to move Friday's deadline to Monday. Hi Sarah, the design needs one more day for the new photos. Could we move the deadline to Monday morning?

## Turn an Idea Into a Story

**Purpose:** Grow a small idea into a story with a character, a problem, and an ending.

```
Help me turn this idea into a short story:

Idea: [your idea in 1-2 sentences]

Give me: a main character with one clear want, the obstacle in their way, a turning point, and an ending that feels earned. Then write the opening paragraph (3-5 sentences) in a [tone: funny / tense / warm] voice. Keep the idea mine — don't replace it with a different one.
```

**Tip:** One want, one obstacle — stories fall apart when the character wants five things at once.

**What to expect:** Mara wants her late grandmother's food stall back. Obstacle: the landlord already sold the spot to a chain. Turning point: she finds her grandmother's old recipe book...

## Summarize a Long Text

**Purpose:** Boil a long article or document down to what actually matters.

```
Summarize this text for me:

[Paste the text]

Give me: 1) a 3-sentence summary, 2) the 5 most important points as bullets, 3) anything the author claims without evidence. Keep the author's meaning — don't add your own opinion.
```

**Tip:** If the text is very long, paste it in parts and ask for a summary of each part first.

**What to expect:** Summary: The article argues remote work raises output but weakens mentoring for juniors. Key points: ... Unsupported claim: 'productivity rose 40%' (no source given).
