# Learning & Study

5 prompts. See [PROMPTING-GUIDE.md](../PROMPTING-GUIDE.md) for how to write your own.

## Explain It Simply, With an Example

**Purpose:** Understand a new topic fast, starting from something you can picture.

```
Explain [topic] to me as if I've never heard of it. Start with a real-life example I can picture, then explain how it works in 3-4 short steps, then tell me one common misunderstanding people have about it. Use simple words. No jargon unless you explain it right away.
```

**Tip:** Ask for an example from something you already know — 'like cooking' or 'like football' makes it stick.

**What to expect:** Think of an API like a waiter in a restaurant: you ask for something, the waiter carries the order to the kitchen, and brings back the food. You never go into the kitchen yourself.

## Build a Study Plan That Fits My Week

**Purpose:** Turn a deadline and a few free hours into a realistic week-by-week plan.

```
Make me a study plan for [subject]. I have [X hours per week], my exam/deadline is on [date], and right now I'm at [beginner / know the basics / almost ready].

Break it into weeks, with 2-3 specific tasks per week. Mix learning, practice, and review — don't put all the practice at the end. Include one rest day per week. Keep it realistic: if the plan needs more hours than I have, say so and cut something.
```

**Tip:** Be honest about your real free hours — a plan built on 10 hours you don't have will fail in week one.

**What to expect:** Week 1: Chapters 1-2 + 20 flashcards. Week 2: Chapters 3-4 + practice problems. Week 3: first full practice test, review every wrong answer.

## Quiz Me Until I Actually Know It

**Purpose:** Test yourself with questions that get harder as you get them right.

```
Quiz me on [topic]. Ask one question at a time and wait for my answer before asking the next one. Start easy, then get harder. When I get one wrong, explain the right answer briefly and ask a similar question later. After 10 questions, tell me my score and the 2 topics I should review most.
```

**Tip:** This works best as a back-and-forth chat — don't ask for all 10 questions at once.

**What to expect:** Q1: What does DNA stand for? ... That's right. Q2 (harder): Which base pairs with adenine? ... Not quite — it's thymine. Here's why...

## Compare Two Things Side by Side

**Purpose:** Decide between two options with a clear table and a straight recommendation.

```
Compare [thing A] and [thing B] for me. I'm trying to decide [what you'll use them for].

Use a table with: what each one is best at, what each one is bad at, cost/effort, and when to pick one over the other. Then give me a straight recommendation for my situation and one sentence why.
```

**Tip:** Say what you'll actually use them for — 'better' means nothing without a use case.

**What to expect:** Recommendation: for a small shop with fixed product fields, SQL — your data has a clear structure and you need reliable reports.

## Teach It Back to Me (Feynman Method)

**Purpose:** Learn deeply by explaining the topic back in your own words.

```
I want to learn [topic] properly. First, explain it to me in simple terms. Then ask me to explain it back to you in my own words. Point out exactly where my explanation is vague or wrong, and explain that part again more simply. Repeat until I can explain the whole thing without notes.
```

**Tip:** The explaining-back part is the whole point — if you skip it, you're just reading, not learning.

**What to expect:** You said 'the heart pumps blood' — good start. But where does the blood go first, and why does that matter? Try again with that part.
