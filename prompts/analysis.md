# Analysis & Research

5 prompts. See [PROMPTING-GUIDE.md](../PROMPTING-GUIDE.md) for how to write your own.

## Pros and Cons for a Real Decision

**Purpose:** Weigh a real decision with honest pros and cons, ranked by how much they matter.

```
Help me decide: [your decision, e.g. 'should I take the job in Jakarta?']. Context: [your situation in 3-4 sentences].

List the real pros and cons — not generic ones. For each point, say how much it matters (big / medium / small) and why. Then tell me: what would have to be true for the 'yes' option to be the right call, and what question should I answer before deciding?
```

**Tip:** Include the costs nobody mentions — time, energy, what you'd give up — not just money.

**What to expect:** Pro (big): 40% higher salary. Con (big): 3 hours commuting a day — that's 15 hours a week you don't get back.

## A SWOT Analysis Without the Fluff

**Purpose:** A focused SWOT that only includes points that would actually change a decision.

```
Do a SWOT analysis for [business / project / idea]. Here's the situation:

[2-4 sentences of context]

Give me 3-4 honest points per box: strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, threats. Skip the obvious filler — only include points that would actually change a decision. End with the single biggest risk and the single best move.
```

**Tip:** A weakness isn't 'we're new' — it's something specific a competitor could use against you.

**What to expect:** Strength: you already have 200 paying customers. Threat: your main supplier just raised prices 20%.

## Check If This Claim Holds Up

**Purpose:** Verify a claim and see what context is missing before you believe or share it.

```
Check this claim for me:

'[Paste the claim]'

Tell me: 1) is it true, partly true, or false — and what's the actual evidence, 2) where the claim probably came from, 3) what important context is missing. If you can't verify it, say so clearly instead of guessing. Use plain language, and name your sources.
```

**Tip:** Paste the exact wording — a claim that says 'most people' is very different from one that says 'everyone'.

**What to expect:** Partly true: coffee does raise alertness, but the study behind the claim had only 12 people and was funded by a coffee brand.

## Summarize a Research Paper

**Purpose:** Get the point of a research paper without wading through the jargon.

```
Summarize this research paper for a non-expert:

[Paste the abstract, or the whole paper if it's short]

Cover: what question the researchers asked, how they tried to answer it, what they found, and what the limits are. Skip the jargon. Tell me in one sentence whether the result actually matters outside the lab.
```

**Tip:** The abstract is usually enough — full papers are mostly method details you'll never need.

**What to expect:** They asked whether sleep affects memory. They tested 60 students and found 8 hours of sleep improved recall by 20%. Limit: all participants were 18-22.

## Find the Weak Spots in This Argument

**Purpose:** Stress-test an argument fairly — steelman it first, then find the real flaws.

```
Find the weak spots in this argument:

[Paste the argument]

Be fair — steelman it first (state the strongest version of the argument), then point out: any claims without evidence, any logical jumps, and any facts that contradict it. For each flaw, say how serious it is and what would fix it.
```

**Tip:** Steelmanning first keeps the critique honest — it's easy to knock down a weak version of someone's point.

**What to expect:** Strongest version: ... Flaw 1 (serious): the argument assumes prices will stay flat, but they rose 15% last year.
