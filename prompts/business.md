# Business & Marketing

5 prompts. See [PROMPTING-GUIDE.md](../PROMPTING-GUIDE.md) for how to write your own.

## Stress-Test My Business Idea

**Purpose:** Find out if a business idea can actually work before spending money on it.

```
Stress-test this business idea for me:

Idea: [describe it in 2-3 sentences]
Target customer: [who]
Price: [how much]

Be honest: 1) who exactly would pay for this, and why, 2) the 3 biggest reasons it could fail, 3) the cheapest way to test it this week, 4) one similar business I should look at. Don't encourage me — tell me what I'd need to believe for this to work.
```

**Tip:** If you can't name a specific person who would pay, the idea isn't ready yet.

**What to expect:** Biggest risk: you're competing with free tools. Cheapest test: a one-page site and 20 cold messages to real shop owners this week.

## Write Copy That Sells Without Shouting

**Purpose:** Write a headline and benefit lines that speak to the customer, not the product.

```
Write marketing copy for [product/service]. It helps [target customer] to [main benefit]. Price: [price].

Give me: a headline (under 10 words), 3 benefit lines that say what the customer gets (not what the product is), and a call to action. Tone: confident, not pushy. No exclamation marks, no 'amazing' or 'revolutionary'.
```

**Tip:** Lead with the customer's problem, not your product's features — nobody buys features.

**What to expect:** Headline: 'WiFi that just works, even in a big house.' Benefit: 'No dead zones in the back rooms.'

## A Week of Social Media Posts

**Purpose:** Get 7 ready post ideas for the week, mixed across formats that actually get read.

```
Give me 7 social media post ideas for [business/topic] for this week. My audience is [who] and I post on [platform].

Mix the formats: 2 useful tips, 2 behind-the-scenes, 1 customer story, 1 question to the audience, 1 offer. For each idea, give me the hook (first line) and 2-3 sentences of content. Keep each post short enough to read in 20 seconds.
```

**Tip:** One idea per post — posts that try to say three things get scrolled past.

**What to expect:** Day 1 (tip): 'Your WiFi is slow in the bedroom for one reason...' Day 3 (behind the scenes): how we install a router in 30 minutes.

## Help Me Price My Product

**Purpose:** Set a price that covers your costs, fits the market, and holds up when customers push back.

```
Help me set a price for [product/service]. It costs me [cost] to deliver, competitors charge [range], and my customers are [who].

Give me: a recommended price and why, a lower 'starter' option and a higher 'premium' option, and what to say when a customer says it's too expensive. If my costs mean the price won't work, say so and show the math.
```

**Tip:** Price on the value to the customer, not just your costs — but never below your costs.

**What to expect:** Recommended: 249,000/month. Your cost is 120,000, competitors are at 300,000 — you can win on price and still keep a healthy margin.

## Build a Customer Persona

**Purpose:** Turn what you know about your customers into a persona you can actually use.

```
Build a customer persona for [product/service]. Here's what I know about my customers so far:

[What you know — even if it's just 2-3 sentences]

Give me: a name and age, what a normal day looks like for them, their biggest frustration related to my product, what they'd Google when looking for a solution, and what would make them choose me over a competitor. Mark anything you're guessing with [guess] so I know what to verify.
```

**Tip:** Base it on real customers you've talked to — a persona built only on guesses will mislead you.

**What to expect:** Rina, 34, runs a small bakery in Bandung. Frustration: her WiFi drops every afternoon when orders come in. [guess] She compares prices on Instagram first.
