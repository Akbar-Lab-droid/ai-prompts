# Job & Career

5 prompts. See [PROMPTING-GUIDE.md](../PROMPTING-GUIDE.md) for how to write your own.

## Turn My Work Into Strong Resume Bullets

**Purpose:** Turn plain job experience into resume bullets with strong verbs and real numbers.

```
Turn this experience into strong resume bullet points:

Role: [job title]
What I did: [2-3 sentences]
Results (if any): [numbers or outcomes]

Give me 3-4 bullets. Start each with a strong verb, include a number where possible, and cut every filler word. If I gave you no results, ask me one question that would help me find a number.
```

**Tip:** Numbers make bullets believable — 'increased sales' is vague, 'increased sales 20% in 3 months' is a result.

**What to expect:** • Cut delivery time 30% by reorganizing the warehouse layout. • Trained 5 new staff in the first month.

## Practice Interview Questions With Me

**Purpose:** Do a mock interview, one question at a time, with honest feedback after each answer.

```
Practice a job interview with me. The role is [job title] at [company type]. Ask me one question at a time and wait for my answer. After each answer, tell me: what was strong, what to improve, and a better way to phrase one part. Mix easy questions with one hard one. After 5 questions, give me an overall score out of 10 and the one thing to fix first.
```

**Tip:** Answer out loud, not in your head — interviews are spoken, and your answer will sound different when you say it.

**What to expect:** Q1: Tell me about yourself. ... Strong: you led with your experience. Improve: cut the 2-minute life story — 60 seconds is enough.

## Write a Cover Letter That Sounds Like Me

**Purpose:** Write a short cover letter that sounds like a real person, not a template.

```
Write a cover letter for [role] at [company]. Here's my background in 3-4 sentences:

[Your background]

And here's why I want this job:

[Your reason]

Keep it under 250 words, 3 short paragraphs. Sound like a real person, not a template — no 'I am writing to express my interest'. Mention one specific thing about the company. End with what I can do for them, not what I want from them.
```

**Tip:** One specific detail about the company beats ten lines of generic praise.

**What to expect:** I saw your team shipped the new checkout in six weeks — that's the kind of pace I work best at, and it's why I want to join.

## A Script for Asking for a Raise

**Purpose:** Prepare a calm script for asking for a raise, with answers to the usual pushbacks.

```
Help me prepare to ask for a raise. My role: [role]. Current salary: [amount]. Market rate: [amount, if you know it]. What I've delivered: [2-3 results].

Give me: a short script I can say out loud (under 150 words), the 3 strongest points to lead with, and answers to the two most likely pushbacks ('we don't have budget', 'let me think about it'). Keep the tone calm and professional.
```

**Tip:** Lead with results, not with how long you've been there — tenure isn't a reason for a raise, impact is.

**What to expect:** Script: 'I want to talk about my compensation. In the last year I...' Pushback: 'We don't have budget' → 'I understand — can we agree on a timeline and what I'd need to deliver for it?'

## Write a LinkedIn About Section

**Purpose:** Write a first-person About section that says what you do in the first line.

```
Write a LinkedIn About section for me. I do [what you do], for [who], and I'm known for [1-2 strengths]. Here's my background:

[2-3 sentences]

Write it in first person, under 150 words. First line should say who I help and how. No buzzwords like 'passionate' or 'results-driven'. End with what kind of work or people I want to hear from.
```

**Tip:** The first line is the only part most people read — make it say what you actually do.

**What to expect:** I help small shops in Bandung get online and sell more — websites, WhatsApp ordering, and simple ads that pay for themselves.
